### pattern-101: V았/었어요
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Take the verb stem. If the last vowel of the stem is ㅏ or ㅗ, add 았어요; all other vowels add 었어요. Contracted stems: 하다 → 했어요; 오다 → 왔어요; 보다 → 봤어요. Irregular ㅂ stems drop ㅂ and add 웠어요 (춥다 → 추웠어요). Irregular ㄷ stems change ㄷ to ㄹ before 었어요 (듣다 → 들었어요). Irregular ㅅ stems drop ㅅ (낫다 → 나았어요). Irregular 르 stems: 모르다 → 몰랐어요, 다르다 → 달랐어요.
- **Examples**:
  - 어제 밥을 먹었어요. (I ate rice yesterday.)
  - 작년에 한국에 갔어요. (I went to Korea last year.)
  - 어릴 때 피아노를 배웠어요. (I learned piano when I was young.)
  - 날씨가 정말 추웠어요. (The weather was really cold.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V-고 있었어요 (past progressive) and V았/었었어요 (remote past / past-perfect nuance). The doubled past 았/었었어요 signals an action that was true in the past but is no longer true: 살았었어요 = 'I used to live there (but no longer do)'. Avoid confusing the ㅂ-irregular past 웠어요 with the future -(으)ㄹ 거예요.

### pattern-102: V-(으)ㄹ 거예요
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Take the verb stem. After a vowel-final stem add -ㄹ 거예요 (가다 → 갈 거예요, 하다 → 할 거예요). After a consonant-final stem add -을 거예요 (먹다 → 먹을 거예요, 읽다 → 읽을 거예요). ㄹ-final stems attach -ㄹ 거예요 directly (살다 → 살 거예요). For first-person use with intention; for third-person it expresses inference about the future.
- **Examples**:
  - 내일 친구를 만날 거예요. (I will meet a friend tomorrow.)
  - 비가 올 거예요. (It will probably rain.)
  - 이 책을 다 읽을 거예요. (I am going to finish reading this book.)
  - 그 사람은 지금 자고 있을 거예요. (That person is probably sleeping right now.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V-(으)ㄹ게요 (first-person promise/commitment only) and A/V-겠- (volition or inference based on sensory evidence). V-(으)ㄹ 거예요 is the general-purpose future and is also used for inference (third person).

### pattern-103: V기 전에
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 기 전에 directly to the plain (dictionary) verb stem regardless of vowel/consonant ending. No tense marking before 전에 — tense is carried by the main clause verb. 먹다 → 먹기 전에; 자다 → 자기 전에; 가다 → 가기 전에.
- **Examples**:
  - 자기 전에 이를 닦아요. (I brush my teeth before sleeping.)
  - 밥을 먹기 전에 손을 씻으세요. (Wash your hands before eating.)
  - 출발하기 전에 지도를 확인했어요. (I checked the map before departing.)
  - 수업 시작하기 전에 숙제를 제출하세요. (Submit the homework before class starts.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V고 나서 (after doing) and V자마자 (as soon as). Also contrast with N 전에 (before a noun/time, e.g., 점심 전에 = before lunch). Do not add tense to 기 전에: 먹었기 전에 is ungrammatical.

### pattern-104: V-습니다 / ㅂ니다
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Declarative: consonant-final stem + 습니다 (먹다 → 먹습니다); vowel-final or ㄹ-final stem + ㅂ니다 (가다 → 갑니다, 살다 → 삽니다 — ㄹ drops). Question: 습니까 / ㅂ니까. Request: -(으)십시오. The highest speech level for formal settings — presentations, announcements, military, customer service.
- **Examples**:
  - 저는 한국어를 공부합니다. (I study Korean. formal)
  - 지금 출발합니까? (Are we departing now? formal)
  - 앉으십시오. (Please be seated. formal)
  - 이 제품은 품질이 우수합니다. (This product is of excellent quality.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with the polite 아/어요 ending (해요체) used in daily conversation. The formal level signals formality of situation, not respect for a person. ㄹ-stem verbs lose the ㄹ before ㅂ니다: 알다 → 압니다.

### pattern-105: -(으)시-
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Insert -(으)시- between the verb stem and the tense/mood ending to honor the subject. After a vowel-final or ㄹ-final stem use -시- directly (가다 → 가세요, 살다 → 사세요). After a consonant-final stem insert -으시- (읽다 → 읽으세요). Some verbs have a separate honorific lexeme: 드시다 (eat/drink), 주무시다 (sleep), 계시다 (be/stay), 돌아가시다 (pass away).
- **Examples**:
  - 선생님이 오세요. (The teacher is coming. honorific)
  - 할머니께서 진지를 드세요. (Grandmother is eating.)
  - 어디에 사세요? (Where do you live? to a respected person)
  - 사장님은 지금 안 계세요. (The director is not in right now.)
- **Notes**: -(으)시- honors the grammatical subject, NOT the listener. Do not apply -(으)시- to first-person subjects. Contrast 있다/없다 (plain) with 계시다 (honorific for people).

### pattern-106: V이/히/리/기다 (passive)
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Four suffixes attach to specific verb stems to create passive verbs: -이- (보다 → 보이다, 쓰다 → 쓰이다), -히- (읽다 → 읽히다, 잡다 → 잡히다), -리- (팔다 → 팔리다, 듣다 → 들리다), -기- (안다 → 안기다, 막다 → 막히다). Each passive verb must be memorized. The agent takes N에게/에 의해; the patient becomes the subject marked by 이/가.
- **Examples**:
  - 문이 닫혔어요. (The door was closed.)
  - 멀리서 음악이 들려요. (Music can be heard from far away.)
  - 그 책은 많이 읽혀요. (That book is read a lot.)
  - 아기가 엄마에게 안겼어요. (The baby was held by the mother.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with the periphrastic passive V-아/어지다 which applies more broadly. Lexical passives sound more natural for specific, idiomatic pairs. Do not confuse with causative suffixes that use overlapping forms.

### pattern-107: V이/히/리/기/우/추/구다 (causative)
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Seven suffixes make a verb mean 'cause someone to do/be X': -이- (먹다 → 먹이다 'feed'), -히- (앉다 → 앉히다 'seat'), -리- (울다 → 울리다 'make cry'), -기- (신다 → 신기다 'put shoes on'), -우- (자다 → 재우다 'put to sleep', 서다 → 세우다), -추- (낮다 → 낮추다 'lower'), -구- (rare). Each causative verb must be memorized. The causee takes N을/를 or N에게.
- **Examples**:
  - 엄마가 아이에게 밥을 먹였어요. (The mother fed the child.)
  - 선생님이 학생들을 앉혔어요. (The teacher seated the students.)
  - 아기를 재웠어요. (I put the baby to sleep.)
  - 소리를 낮춰 주세요. (Please lower the volume.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with the periphrastic causative V-게 하다 (make/let someone do), which is productive and applies to any verb. Lexical causatives are restricted to specific verb stems and sound more natural for those pairs.

### pattern-108: N에게 / 한테 / 께
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach directly to a noun. 에게 is written/formal; 한테 is colloquial; 께 is honorific. Direction 'to': 친구에게 편지를 써요. Direction 'from': add 서 to the dative — 에게서 / 한테서 / 께서. For non-person recipients use N에 not N에게.
- **Examples**:
  - 친구에게 문자를 보냈어요. (I sent a text to a friend.)
  - 선생님께 여쭤볼게요. (I will ask the teacher. honorific)
  - 강아지한테 밥을 줬어요. (I gave food to the dog.)
  - 부모님께서 용돈을 주셨어요. (My parents gave me pocket money.)
- **Notes**: Distinguish N에게서 (from a person) from N에서 (from a place). The particle 한테 is identical in function to 에게 but is conversational — do not use in formal writing.

### pattern-109: N와/과 / 하고 / (이)랑
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: 와 attaches after a vowel-final noun; 과 after a consonant-final noun. 하고 attaches to any noun (colloquial). (이)랑: 랑 after vowel-final, 이랑 after consonant-final (very informal). All link nouns ('N and N') or mark accompaniment ('with N').
- **Examples**:
  - 사과와 배를 샀어요. (I bought apples and pears. written)
  - 친구하고 영화를 봤어요. (I watched a movie with a friend. spoken)
  - 오빠랑 언니랑 같이 갔어요. (I went with my older brother and older sister.)
  - 밥과 국을 먹었어요. (I ate rice and soup. formal/written)
- **Notes**: These particles connect nouns, NOT clauses or verbs. To connect verbs use V고. Contrast accompaniment 'with N' versus the instrument/manner particle (으)로.

### pattern-110: N밖에 + 부정
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 밖에 directly to a noun. The predicate MUST be negative: 없다, 안 V, 못 V, V지 않다. N밖에 없어요 = 'there is only N / nothing but N'. Using 밖에 with a positive predicate is ungrammatical.
- **Examples**:
  - 돈이 천 원밖에 없어요. (I only have 1,000 won.)
  - 이 방법밖에 없어요. (There is no other way.)
  - 한 명밖에 안 왔어요. (Only one person came.)
  - 물밖에 못 마셔요. (I can only drink water.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with N만 (only N, takes a positive predicate). N밖에 emphasizes scarcity or restriction — the speaker feels the amount is insufficient. 밖에 없다 is very common and should be memorized as a chunk.

### pattern-111: N이나 (approximation / 'about N')
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 이나 after a consonant-final noun/numeral, 나 after a vowel-final noun/numeral. Used after numbers or quantity words to express 'as many/much as', often with surprise at the large quantity. When 이나 follows a question word (뭐, 어디, 누구) it becomes an indefinite: 뭐나 = anything.
- **Examples**:
  - 사람이 백 명이나 왔어요. (As many as 100 people came.)
  - 세 시간이나 기다렸어요. (I waited for three whole hours.)
  - 커피를 다섯 잔이나 마셨어요. (I drank as many as five cups of coffee.)
  - 거리가 10킬로나 돼요. (The distance is as far as 10 kilometers.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with 밖에 + negative (scarcity/only) versus 이나 (surprising abundance). Also contrast with (이)나 as a concessive 'N or alternatively' (see pattern-112).

### pattern-112: N이라도
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Attach 이라도 after a consonant-final noun, 라도 after a vowel-final noun. Expresses 'even if only N' or 'at least N (since something better isn't available)': a concessive minimum. Frequently used with 한잔, 잠깐, etc.
- **Examples**:
  - 차라도 한 잔 드세요. (At least have a cup of tea.)
  - 잠깐이라도 만나고 싶어요. (I want to meet you even if just for a moment.)
  - 물이라도 드릴까요? (Should I at least bring you some water?)
  - 오늘 안 되면 내일이라도 와 주세요. (If today doesn't work, please come at least tomorrow.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with N이나 (approximation or concessive 'or') and N만 (only). 이라도 specifically signals that the speaker is settling for less than ideal. V-아/어도 is the verbal counterpart.

### pattern-113: -네요
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 네요 to a verb/adjective stem as a sentence-final ending. For action verbs: stem + 네요 (가다 → 가네요). For descriptive verbs: stem + 네요 (맛있다 → 맛있네요). For 이다: N이네요. Expresses that the speaker has just noticed, realized, or discovered something — new information. Past form: -았/었네요.
- **Examples**:
  - 정말 맛있네요! (Oh, this is really delicious! — just tasted it)
  - 벌써 도착했네요. (Oh, you've already arrived!)
  - 오늘 날씨가 좋네요. (Oh, the weather is nice today. noticing)
  - 한국어를 잘하시네요. (Oh, you speak Korean well! impressed realization)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -군요/구나 (pattern-114) — both express discovery but -네요 implies direct sensory perception, while -군요 is slightly more intellectual/deductive. -네요 is polite; -네 is casual. Do not use -네요 for information you already knew before the conversation.

### pattern-114: -군요 / -구나
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Polite form: verb/adjective stem + 군요. For action verbs in present tense add 는 before 군요: V-는군요. For descriptive verbs attach directly: A-군요. Casual/intimate form: -구나. Past: V/A-았/었군요. Expresses the speaker's sudden realization or surprise — often a monologue-type exclamation.
- **Examples**:
  - 그렇군요. 몰랐어요. (Oh, I see. I didn't know that.)
  - 비가 오는군요. (Oh, it's raining!)
  - 아, 학생이구나! (Oh, you're a student! — casual, to oneself)
  - 많이 힘들었군요. (Oh, that must have been really hard for you.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -네요 (pattern-113): -네요 is more common in everyday speech and implies direct sensory discovery; -군요 is slightly more literary/formal. -구나 is used in casual speech or when talking to oneself / to a child. 그렇군요 is a very common filler meaning 'I see / is that so?'.

### pattern-115: -(으)ㄹ걸요
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Attach -(으)ㄹ걸요 as a sentence-final ending. After vowel-final or ㄹ-final stems use -ㄹ걸요; after consonant-final stems use -을걸요. Two uses: (1) Conjecture about the present or future (hedged prediction); (2) Regret about a past action not done: -았/었을걸요. Casual form (no 요): -(으)ㄹ걸.
- **Examples**:
  - 지금쯤 도착했을걸요. (I think they've probably arrived by now.)
  - 그 식당은 비쌀걸요. (That restaurant is probably expensive.)
  - 일찍 출발할걸 그랬어. (I should have left earlier. regret, casual)
  - 내일 비가 올걸요. (I think it will rain tomorrow.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -(으)ㄹ 것 같아요 (more explicit conjecture) and -겠어요 (inference from evidence). The regret use is typically in casual speech and often followed by 그랬어 for emphasis.

### pattern-116: V았/었으면 좋겠어요
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Form: V-았으면 좋겠어요 (after ㅏ/ㅗ vowel stem) or V-었으면 좋겠어요 (after other vowels); 하다 → 했으면 좋겠어요. Despite the past tense morpheme, the meaning is present/future wish or hypothesis — this is a counterfactual conditional. Can be shortened to V았/었으면 해요 colloquially.
- **Examples**:
  - 날씨가 좋았으면 좋겠어요. (I wish the weather were nice.)
  - 빨리 나았으면 좋겠어요. (I hope you get better soon.)
  - 복권에 당첨됐으면 좋겠어요. (I wish I could win the lottery.)
  - 같이 갈 수 있었으면 좋겠어요. (I wish I could go together with you.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V고 싶어요 (I want to do V, for one's own desire to perform an action). 았/었으면 좋겠어요 expresses a broader wish about states or situations, including about others. Also used for polite requests: 조용히 해 주셨으면 좋겠어요. The 았/었 is NOT past tense here — it is a counterfactual mood marker.

### pattern-117: V-는지 / (으)ㄴ지 / (으)ㄹ지
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Embedded question endings transforming a question into a subordinate clause. Action verb present: -는지. Descriptive verb: -(으)ㄴ지. Past: -았/었는지. Future/conjecture: -(으)ㄹ지. Copula: N인지. Common collocations: 모르다, 알다, 궁금하다, 물어보다.
- **Examples**:
  - 뭘 먹을지 모르겠어요. (I don't know what to eat.)
  - 그 사람이 어디 있는지 알아요? (Do you know where that person is?)
  - 날씨가 좋은지 확인해 보세요. (Check whether the weather is good.)
  - 언제 도착할지 궁금해요. (I am curious when they will arrive.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V-(으)ㄴ/는데 (background clause) and V-아/어서 (reason clause). The -는지 pattern specifically embeds yes/no or wh-questions into indirect speech or thought. Do not confuse -(으)ㄹ지 with -(으)ㄹ까 (tag question / self-addressed question).

### pattern-118: N의
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 의 to a noun to mark possession or modification: N의 N = 'N's N'. In spoken Korean 의 is very commonly contracted: 나의 → 내, 너의 → 네/니, 저의 → 제. Noun-noun compounds without 의 are common in speech: 친구 집 = 친구의 집.
- **Examples**:
  - 제 친구의 책이에요. (It is my friend's book.)
  - 한국의 수도는 서울이에요. (The capital of Korea is Seoul.)
  - 내 생각에는 맞는 것 같아요. (In my opinion, it seems right.)
  - 오늘의 날씨는 맑아요. (Today's weather is clear.)
- **Notes**: In daily speech 의 is often dropped and nouns simply juxtaposed: 친구 가방 = 친구의 가방. The contracted pronouns 내 (my), 네/니 (your), 제 (my-formal) must be memorized as set forms. 의 is NOT used to form adjectives from nouns — Korean uses A-ㄴ/은 for that.

### pattern-119: 수 분 개 명 권 ... (단위 명사)
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Korean quantities use native or Sino-Korean numbers plus a counter. Sino-Korean counters: 분 (minutes), 층 (floor), 번 (number/times), 월 (month), 원 (won). Native Korean counters: 개 (generic objects), 명 (people-formal), 분 (people-honorific), 마리 (animals), 잔 (cups/glasses), 권 (books), 병 (bottles), 장 (flat sheets), 켤레 (pairs), 벌 (suits of clothing), 대 (machines/vehicles), 채 (buildings). Number + counter come after the noun: 사과 두 개.
- **Examples**:
  - 사과 세 개 주세요. (Please give me three apples.)
  - 학생이 스무 명 있어요. (There are twenty students.)
  - 커피 두 잔 마셨어요. (I drank two cups of coffee.)
  - 책 한 권을 빌렸어요. (I borrowed one book.)
- **Notes**: Native Korean numbers 1-4 and 20 contract before counters: 하나 → 한, 둘 → 두, 셋 → 세, 넷 → 네, 스물 → 스무. 명 is standard; 분 is honorific. 번 with Sino-Korean gives ordinals with 째: 세 번째 = third time.

### pattern-120: ㅂ 불규칙
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Verbs/adjectives whose stem ends in ㅂ: when the next syllable begins with a vowel, ㅂ changes to 우 (돕다 → 도와, 곱다 → 고와 are exceptions using 오). So 춥다 + 어요 → 추워요. Common irregular: 덥다 → 더워요, 맵다 → 매워요, 쉽다 → 쉬워요, 어렵다 → 어려워요, 가깝다 → 가까워요. Exceptions (regular): 입다 → 입어요, 잡다 → 잡아요.
- **Examples**:
  - 날씨가 추워요. (The weather is cold. 춥다 + 어요)
  - 한국어가 어려워요. (Korean is difficult. 어렵다 + 어요)
  - 이 음식이 매워요. (This food is spicy. 맵다 + 어요)
  - 오늘 쉬워요. (It is easy today. 쉽다 + 어요)
- **Notes**: The ㅂ irregular applies before vowel-initial endings: -아/어요, -아/어서, -(으)면, -(으)ㄹ. Before consonant-initial endings ㅂ is regular: 춥습니다. If the stem ends in ㅂ and is a descriptive adjective, it is almost certainly ㅂ-irregular. Regular ㅂ verbs (잡다, 입다) are typically active action verbs.

### pattern-121: ㄷ 불규칙
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Verbs whose stem ends in ㄷ: when the next syllable begins with a vowel, ㄷ changes to ㄹ. Common ㄷ-irregular: 듣다 → 들어요, 걷다 → 걸어요, 묻다 (ask) → 물어요, 싣다 → 실어요. Regular ㄷ verbs (ㄷ does NOT change): 닫다 → 닫아요, 믿다 → 믿어요, 받다 → 받아요. The irregular applies before vowel-initial endings only.
- **Examples**:
  - 음악을 들어요. (I listen to music. 듣다 irregular)
  - 공원을 걸었어요. (I walked in the park. 걷다 irregular)
  - 문을 닫아요. (I close the door. 닫다 regular)
  - 선생님께 물어볼게요. (I will ask the teacher. 묻다 irregular)
- **Notes**: The ㄷ irregular set is small — memorize: 듣다, 걷다, 묻다 (ask), 싣다. Regular ㄷ verbs include 닫다, 믿다, 받다. Note that 묻다 (bury) is regular (묻어요), while 묻다 (ask) is irregular (물어요) — disambiguate by context.

### pattern-122: ㅅ 불규칙
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Verbs/adjectives whose stem ends in ㅅ: when the next syllable begins with a vowel, ㅅ drops entirely. Common ㅅ-irregular: 낫다 → 나아요, 잇다 → 이어요, 짓다 → 지어요, 붓다 → 부어요. Regular ㅅ verbs: 웃다 → 웃어요, 벗다 → 벗어요, 씻다 → 씻어요. Before consonant-initial endings all ㅅ verbs are regular.
- **Examples**:
  - 빨리 나으면 좋겠어요. (I hope you get better quickly. 낫다 irregular)
  - 집을 지었어요. (They built a house. 짓다 irregular)
  - 크게 웃었어요. (I laughed loudly. 웃다 regular)
  - 선을 이어요. (Connect the lines. 잇다 irregular)
- **Notes**: The key test: if dropping ㅅ leaves a single vowel that makes sense, it is probably irregular (낫다 → 나+어요 = 나아요). Regular ㅅ verbs tend to be more physically concrete/observable actions. Note that 낫다 (get better) must be distinguished from 낮다 (be low), which is regular.

### pattern-123: 르 불규칙
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Verbs/adjectives whose stem ends in 르: when the next syllable is 아요 or 어요, ㄹ is doubled onto the preceding syllable and 으 is dropped: 모르다 → 몰라요, 다르다 → 달라요, 빠르다 → 빨라요, 부르다 → 불러요, 고르다 → 골라요, 자르다 → 잘라요. All 르 verbs follow this rule; there are no regular exceptions among 르 verbs.
- **Examples**:
  - 그 이름을 몰라요. (I don't know that name. 모르다 → 몰라요)
  - 두 사람이 달라요. (The two people are different. 다르다 → 달라요)
  - 노래를 불렀어요. (I sang a song. 부르다 → 불렀어요)
  - 종이를 잘라요. (Cut the paper. 자르다 → 잘라요)
- **Notes**: Do not confuse 르 irregular with ㄹ-final stems: 살다 (ㄹ-final) → 살아요 (regular); 모르다 (르-irregular) → 몰라요. The 르 irregular ONLY applies before -아/어 endings — before consonant-initial endings: 모릅니다, 다릅니다.

### pattern-124: ㅎ 불규칙
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Adjectives whose stem ends in ㅎ: before -아/어요, ㅎ drops and ending becomes 애요: 빨갛다 → 빨개요, 파랗다 → 파래요, 노랗다 → 노래요, 하얗다 → 하얘요, 그렇다 → 그래요, 이렇다 → 이래요, 저렇다 → 저래요, 어떻다 → 어때요. Before -(으)ㄴ (noun-modifying): ㅎ drops and no additional vowel: 빨간, 파란, 하얀. Conjugation in -게, -고, 습니다 is regular.
- **Examples**:
  - 하늘이 파래요. (The sky is blue. 파랗다 → 파래요)
  - 장미가 빨개요. (The rose is red. 빨갛다 → 빨개요)
  - 그 사람은 어때요? (How is that person? 어떻다 → 어때요)
  - 하얀 눈이 내려요. (White snow is falling. 하얗다 → 하얀 + 눈)
- **Notes**: ㅎ irregular only applies to color adjectives and demonstrative adjectives (이렇다, 그렇다, 저렇다, 어떻다). Action verbs with ㅎ (넣다, 닿다) are regular: 넣어요, 닿아요. The noun-modifying form (파란 하늘, 빨간 사과) should be memorized as vocabulary chunks.

### pattern-125: A/V-겠-
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Insert 겠 between the verb stem and the sentence-final ending. Two main uses: (1) First-person volition/intention (stronger than -(으)ㄹ 거예요): 하겠어요 = 'I will do it (I commit to it)'; (2) Inference from direct sensory evidence: 맛있겠다 = 'that looks delicious'. Fixed expressions: 알겠어요/알겠습니다 = 'I understand', 모르겠어요 = 'I don't know / I'm not sure'.
- **Examples**:
  - 열심히 하겠습니다. (I will do my best. formal commitment)
  - 맛있겠다! (That looks delicious! inference from sight)
  - 네, 알겠어요. (Yes, I understand. fixed polite expression)
  - 거기 너무 춥겠어요. (It must be really cold there.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -(으)ㄹ 거예요 (factual future) and -(으)ㄹ 것 같아요 (hedged conjecture). -겠- is more immediate and emotionally grounded. In formal speech the volition use of -겠습니다 replaces -(으)ㄹ 거예요 for polite commitment: 도와드리겠습니다.

### pattern-126: V-(으)ㄴ/는데
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Attaches to a verb or adjective stem as a connective ending providing background context, scene-setting, or mild contrast for the main clause. Action verb present: -는데. Action verb past: -(으)ㄴ데 (먹었는데). Descriptive verb: -(으)ㄴ데. N이다: N인데. Multiple functions: background, contrast, mild complaint, topic-opening.
- **Examples**:
  - 배고픈데 뭐 먹을까요? (I'm hungry — what should we eat?)
  - 이 영화는 재미있는데 좀 길어요. (This movie is interesting, but it's a bit long.)
  - 어제 친구를 만났는데 너무 반가웠어요. (I met a friend yesterday — it was so nice to see them.)
  - 사실은 저도 모르는데요. (Actually, I don't know either, you see.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -지만 (explicit contrast), -아/어서 (reason), and -고 (sequential listing). -는데 is more versatile and softening. As a sentence-final ending (-는데요) it softens a statement or implies an unsaid continuation.

---

### pattern-127: 따라서 / 그러나 / 반면에 / 즉 / 결국
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Sentence-initial discourse connectors for formal writing, essays, and academic discourse. 따라서 (therefore/accordingly); 그러나 (however — formal written contrast); 반면에 (on the other hand — explicit two-sided contrast); 즉 (that is/namely — introduces restatement or definition); 결국 (in the end/ultimately — summarizes or reaches conclusion). All appear at the start of a sentence or clause. In spoken Korean 그래서/그런데 are preferred; these written forms signal formal register.
- **Examples**:
  - 연습이 중요하다. 따라서 매일 해야 한다. (Practice is important. Therefore, you must do it every day.)
  - 그는 열심히 했다. 그러나 결과가 좋지 않았다. (He worked hard. However, the result was not good.)
  - 두 방법은 다르다. 반면에 목표는 같다. (The two methods are different. On the other hand, the goal is the same.)
  - 이것이 즉, 핵심이다. (This is, namely, the key point.)
- **Notes**: These are discourse-level connectors, not grammatical endings — they do not conjugate. Contrast with connective endings that link clauses within a sentence (-아서, -지만, -고). In TOPIK writing tasks and academic Korean these connectors are expected.

### pattern-128: V/A-아/어지다
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach -아/어지다 to a verb/adjective stem following vowel harmony (ㅏ/ㅗ → 아지다; other vowels → 어지다; 하다 → 해지다). Two functions: (1) Stative change with descriptive verbs: 좋아지다 (get better), 나빠지다 (get worse), 커지다 (get bigger), 따뜻해지다 (get warm); (2) Periphrastic passive with action verbs where no lexical passive exists: 만들다 → 만들어지다.
- **Examples**:
  - 날씨가 따뜻해졌어요. (The weather has gotten warm.)
  - 한국어 실력이 좋아졌어요. (My Korean ability has improved.)
  - 이 제품은 한국에서 만들어졌어요. (This product was made in Korea.)
  - 마음이 편해졌어요. (My heart/mind has become at ease.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V-게 되다 — both express change but 아/어지다 focuses on gradual or automatic change of state, while -게 되다 focuses on circumstantial change or transition in situation. 아/어지다 with descriptive verbs is one of the most productive patterns for expressing change.

---

### pattern-129: V-아/어 놓다 / 두다
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Attach -아/어 놓다 or -아/어 두다 following vowel harmony. Both mean to perform an action and leave the result in place for future use or reference. 놓다 and 두다 are largely interchangeable; 놓다 can emphasize the act of placing, 두다 the act of keeping/storing. Common with preparatory actions: 사 놓다 (buy and keep), 준비해 두다 (prepare in advance), 써 놓다 (write down).
- **Examples**:
  - 내일을 위해 밥을 미리 만들어 놨어요. (I made rice in advance for tomorrow.)
  - 문을 열어 두세요. (Please leave the door open.)
  - 중요한 내용은 적어 놓으세요. (Write down the important content and keep it.)
  - 짐을 여기에 놔 두면 돼요. (You can leave the luggage here.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -아/어 있다 (describes ongoing resulting state) versus 놓다/두다 (the action of creating that state intentionally). Contrast with -아/어 버리다 (complete and done with, often with regret or relief) versus 놓다/두다 (complete and preserved for use).

---

### pattern-130: V/A-다면 / N이라면
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Attaches as a connective ending expressing a hypothetical or counterfactual condition — stronger than plain -(으)면. Action verb present: -다면 (간다면, 먹는다면). Descriptive verb: -다면 (좋다면). Past: -았/었다면. N이다: N이라면 (after consonant), N라면 (after vowel). Often co-occurs with -겠다 or -(으)ㄹ 텐데 in the result clause.
- **Examples**:
  - 내가 새라면 날아갈 텐데. (If I were a bird, I would fly away.)
  - 돈이 많다면 뭘 하겠어요? (If you had lots of money, what would you do?)
  - 그때 말했다면 달라졌을 거예요. (If you had spoken up then, things would have changed.)
  - 학생이라면 할인이 돼요. (If you are a student, there is a discount.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -(으)면 (neutral real-world conditional) and -아/어도 (concessive conditional 'even if'). 다면 is often used in thought experiments, persuasive writing, and emotional/philosophical statements. N이라면 is very common in advice: 나라면 = 'if I were you'.

---

### pattern-131: V-고 있다
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach -고 있다 to the plain verb stem regardless of ending. Conjugate 있다 for tense/speech level: 고 있어요 (polite present), 고 있었어요 (polite past progressive). Different from -아/어 있다 (stative result): 앉고 있어요 = 'in the process of sitting down', while 앉아 있어요 = 'is (already) seated'.
- **Examples**:
  - 지금 밥을 먹고 있어요. (I am eating right now.)
  - 그 사람은 한국어를 공부하고 있어요. (That person is studying Korean.)
  - 비가 오고 있었어요. (It was raining. past progressive)
  - 뭐 하고 있어요? (What are you doing?)
- **Notes**: Closely related to V-는 중이에요 — both express ongoing action. 고 있어요 is more colloquial; 는 중이에요 is slightly more formal and emphasizes that the action is midway. Contrast with -아/어 있다 (resultant state) — choose based on whether the focus is action-in-progress or resulting state.

---

### pattern-132: A/V-아/어 보이다
- **Level**: B1
- **Usage**: Attach -아/어 보이다 to a descriptive verb (adjective) stem following vowel harmony. Creates an impression based on visual appearance: 젊어 보이다 (look young), 비싸 보이다 (look expensive), 피곤해 보이다 (look tired). The subject is the thing or person being observed.
- **Examples**:
  - 오늘 피곤해 보여요. (You look tired today.)
  - 이 옷이 비싸 보여요. (This outfit looks expensive.)
  - 그 사람이 젊어 보여요. (That person looks young.)
  - 날씨가 좋아 보이는데 나갈까요? (The weather looks nice — shall we go out?)
- **Notes**: Contrast with N 같아요 ('seems like N', based on resemblance) and -(으)ㄹ 것 같아요 (hedged conjecture). 아/어 보이다 is specifically visual/perceptual. For non-visual impressions use 들리다 (be heard) or 느껴지다 (be felt). Verify against the existing pattern list to avoid duplication with pattern-079.

### pattern-133: ㅂ-변칙 — ㅂ → 워 (dedicated drill entry)
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: When a stem ends in ㅂ (e.g. 덥-), replace ㅂ with 우 before any vowel-initial ending: 덥 + 어요 → 더 + 우 + 어요 → 더워요. Applies to most descriptive verbs ending in ㅂ. Exceptions: 잡다 / 좁다 / 입다 keep ㅂ.
- **Examples**:

  - 이 문제가 어려워요. (This problem is difficult.)
  - 길이 가까워요. (The road is close.)
  - 할 수 있으니까 쉬워요. (It is easy since you can do it.)
- **Notes**: Cross-reference with pattern-120. This entry provides the dedicated conjugation-rule drill frame for the ㅂ irregular specifically.

### pattern-134: ㄷ-변칙 — ㄷ → ㄹ before vowels (dedicated drill entry)
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: When a stem ends in ㄷ (e.g. 듣-), change ㄷ → ㄹ before any vowel-initial ending: 듣 + 어요 → 들어요. Exceptions: 받다 / 믿다 / 닫다 keep ㄷ.
- **Examples**:

  - 공원을 걸어요. (I walk in the park.)
  - 짐을 실어요. (Load the luggage.)
  - 전화를 물어봐요. (Ask by phone.)
- **Notes**: Cross-reference with pattern-121.

### pattern-135: ㅅ-변칙 — ㅅ drops before vowels (dedicated drill entry)
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: When a stem ends in ㅅ (e.g. 짓-), drop ㅅ before any vowel-initial ending: 짓 + 어요 → 지어요. Exceptions: 벗다 / 씻다 / 빼앗다 keep ㅅ.
- **Examples**:
  - 집을 지어요. (They build a house.)
  - 빨리 나아요. (I get better quickly.)
  - 선을 이어요. (Connect the lines.)
  - 물이 부어요. (Pour the water.)
- **Notes**: Cross-reference with pattern-122.

### pattern-136: ㄹ-탈락 — ㄹ drops before ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Stems ending in ㄹ (e.g. 알-) drop ㄹ before -(으)ㄴ, -(으)ㄹ, -ㅂ니다, -세요, -ㄴ다: 알 + ㄴ → 아는, 알 + ㅂ니다 → 압니다, 알 + 세요 → 아세요. Before vowel endings ㄹ is retained normally: 알 + 아요 → 알아요.
- **Examples**:
  - 그 사람을 알아요? (Do you know that person?)
  - 압니다. (I know. formal)
  - 아세요? (Do you know? honorific)
  - 서울에 살아요. (I live in Seoul.)
- **Notes**: This is not a true "irregular" — ㄹ-final stems are entirely predictable. The drop occurs specifically before the consonants ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ. Before all other consonants and before vowels, ㄹ is retained.

### pattern-137: ㅎ-변칙 — ㅎ drops + vowel contracts (dedicated drill entry)
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Adjective stems ending in ㅎ (e.g. 빨갛-) drop ㅎ and the stem vowel ㅏ/ㅓ contracts with the ending vowel ㅏ/ㅓ → ㅐ: 빨갛 + 아요 → 빨가 + 아요 → 빨개요. ㅎ also drops before -(으)ㄴ: 빨갛 + 은 → 빨간. Exceptions: 좋다 / 넣다 keep ㅎ.
- **Examples**:
  - 빨간 사과를 주세요. (Please give me a red apple.)
  - 하늘이 파래요. (The sky is blue.)
  - 그게 어때요? (How is that?)
  - 노란 꽃이에요. (It is a yellow flower.)
- **Notes**: Cross-reference with pattern-124.

### pattern-138: 르-변칙 — 르 → ㄹㄹ before 아/어 (dedicated drill entry)
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Stems ending in 르 (e.g. 모르-) replace 르 with ㄹㄹ and drop 으 before 아/어 endings: 모르 + 아요 → 몰 + 라요 → 몰라요. The pattern: drop 으, add ㄹ to the preceding syllable as a final consonant, then add 라/러.
- **Examples**:
  - 몰라요. (I don't know.)
  - 달라요. (It is different.)
  - 불렀어요. (I called / sang.)
  - 빨라요! (It's fast!)
- **Notes**: Cross-reference with pattern-123.

### pattern-139: N도
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 도 directly to a noun, replacing 은/는/이/가/을/를. 저도 = 'me too'. When stacking on place/time particles, 도 follows: 학교에도 갔어요 'went to school too'.
- **Examples**:
  - 저도 알아요. (I also know.)
  - 오빠도 왔어요. (My older brother came too.)
  - 여기에도 있어요. (It is here too.)
  - 한국어도 배워요. (I also learn Korean.)
- **Notes**: 도 stacks on place/direction particles but replaces subject/object/topic particles. N도 N도 = 'both N and N'. Contrast with 만 (only/just).

---

### pattern-140: N만
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: Attach 만 directly to the noun. It replaces 은/는/이/가/을/를 but follows place/time particles: 여기서만 'only here'. Contrast with 밖에: 만 can appear in positive sentences, 밖에 cannot.
- **Examples**:
  - 저만 왔어요. (Only I came.)
  - 물만 마셔요. (I only drink water.)
  - 여기서만 써요. (Use it only here.)
  - 한 번만 더 해요. (Do it just one more time.)
- **Notes**: 만 is neutral — it does not express surprise or judgment about the quantity. Compare: 물만 마셔요 (I drink only water, neutral) vs. 물밖에 못 마셔요 (I can drink nothing but water, implies restriction/hardship).

### pattern-141: N이었어요 / 였어요
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: N이었어요 after consonant-final nouns; N였어요 after vowel-final nouns. 아니다 past: 아니었어요. This is not 이에요 + past marker; it is the full past conjugation of the copula verb 이다.
- **Examples**:
  - 저는 학생이었어요. (I was a student.)
  - 선생님이었어요. (I was a teacher.)
  - 친구였어요. (They were my friend.)
  - 학생이 아니었어요. (I was not a student.)
- **Notes**: The copula past 이었어요/였어요 is the basis for several connective forms: 이었는데/였는데 (background), 이었으니까/였으니까 (reason). Formal equivalent: 이었습니다/였습니다.

### pattern-142: N인데
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: N이다 + -(으)ㄴ데 = 인데 (always 인데 regardless of final consonant). Provides background information, mild contrast, or softened statement before a main clause.
- **Examples**:
  - 저는 학생인데요, 질문이 있어요. (I'm a student, and I have a question.)
  - 이건 선물인데 마음에 드세요? (This is a gift — do you like it?)
  - 오늘 주말인데 뭐 해요? (It's the weekend — what are you doing?)
  - 저는 외국인인데 괜찮아요? (I'm a foreigner — is that okay?)
- **Notes**: 인데 cannot be used as a final sentence-ending for forceful statements; it implies continuation or an expected response. Polite softening form: 인데요 (with 요).

### pattern-143: N이라서 / 라서
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: N이다 + 아/어서 → 이라서 (consonant-final) / 라서 (vowel-final). States a cause based on what the subject IS rather than what it does.
- **Examples**:
  - 학생이라서 할인이 돼요. (Because I'm a student, I get a discount.)
  - 주말이라서 바빠요. (Because it's the weekend, I'm busy.)
  - 외국인이라서 모를 수 있어요. (Because I'm a foreigner, I may not know.)
  - 아침이라서 사람이 없어요. (Because it's morning, there are no people.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with 이니까/니까 (N이니까 is a stronger, more certain reason-giver that often precedes commands or suggestions). 이라서 is softer and more explanatory.

### pattern-144: N이니까 / 니까
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: N이다 + (으)니까 → 이니까 (consonant-final) / 니까 (vowel-final). Stronger/more certain reason than 이라서, often followed by a conclusion or suggestion.
- **Examples**:
  - 제가 외국인이니까 모르는 게 당연해요. (Since I'm a foreigner, it's natural not to know.)
  - 선배니까 먼저 드세요. (Since you're my senior, please eat first.)
  - 학생이니까 열심히 해야 해요. (Since you're a student, you have to work hard.)
  - 주말이니까 쉬어도 돼요. (Since it's the weekend, you can rest.)
- **Notes**: 이니까 is a grammatical form of the causal connector 으니까 applied to the copula. Contrast with 이라서 (softer, more explanatory) and N 때문에 (more neutral cause-attribution without strong assertion).

### pattern-145: 저희 가족은 ~ 명이에요
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: 저희 (humble 'our/my') + 가족은 + native number + 명이에요. This integrates counter (명), copula (이에요), and humble pronoun in one core sentence frame for describing family size.
- **Examples**:
  - 저희 가족은 네 명이에요. (My family has four people.)
  - 저희 가족은 다섯 명이에요. (My family has five people.)
  - 저희 가족이 몇 명이에요? (How many people are in your family?)
  - 저희 가족은 세 명인데 저, 어머니, 아버지예요. (My family has three people — me, my mother, and my father.)
- **Notes**: 저희 (humble plural 'our') is used instead of 우리 in formal/polite contexts when speaking to someone outside the family group. The counter 명 (for people) takes native Korean numbers: 한, 두, 세, 네, 다섯...

### pattern-146: N께서 V으시다
- **Level**: A2
- **Usage**: 께서 replaces 이/가 when the subject is a person you honor (parents, teachers, elders, customers). It co-occurs with the honorific verb suffix -(으)시-: 어머니께서 오셨어요. Do not use 께서 for your own actions.
- **Examples**:
  - 어머니께서 오셨어요. (My mother came. honorific)
  - 선생님께서 말씀하셨어요. (The teacher said. honorific)
  - 할아버지께서 주무세요. (Grandfather is sleeping. honorific)
  - 사장님께서 회의에 계세요. (The director is in a meeting. honorific)
- **Notes**: 께서 is formal/written; 이/가 with -(으)시- is also acceptable in speech: 선생님이 오셨어요. 께 (without 서) is the dative particle 'to a respected person'; 께서 (with 서) is the subject particle. Do not confuse them.

## B2/C1 Advanced Patterns (from specialist part-2)

### pattern-147: V-이/히/리/기/우/구/추-다 (사동) — morphological causative
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Express that the subject causes another person or animal to perform the base verb action. Choose suffix allomorph by stem type: -이- (먹다 → 먹이다, 죽다 → 죽이다), -히- (입다 → 입히다, 읽다 → 읽히다, 앉다 → 앉히다), -기- (웃다 → 웃기다, 숨다 → 숨기다), -리- (울다 → 울리다, 알다 → 알리다), -우- (자다 → 재우다, 서다 → 세우다), -추- (낮다 → 낮추다), -구- (rare). The causee takes N을/를 or N에게.
- **Examples**:
  - 아이에게 밥을 먹였어요. (I fed the child.)
  - 아기에게 옷을 입혔어요. (I dressed the baby.)
  - 그 농담이 모두를 웃겼어요. (That joke made everyone laugh.)
  - 그 영화가 저를 울렸어요. (That film made me cry.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with passive suffixes that share the same forms (-이-, -히-, -리-, -기-): context and meaning distinguish them. 먹이다 = feed (causative); 먹히다 = be eaten (passive). Causative: an animate causer makes someone act. Passive: the subject is acted upon.

### pattern-148: V-게 하다
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Neutral indirect causation applicable to any verb; marks the causee with 에게/한테. The most productive causative form. Applies when no morphological causative suffix exists, when the causer is more distant/indirect, or as the default for compound verbs.
- **Examples**:
  - 선생님이 학생들에게 책을 읽게 했어요. (The teacher had the students read the book.)
  - 부모님이 저를 공부하게 했어요. (My parents made me study.)
  - 회사에서 직원들을 야근하게 해요. (The company makes employees work overtime.)
  - 음악이 사람들을 춤추게 해요. (Music makes people dance.)
- **Notes**: Neutral in tone — the caused agent may have had a choice. Contrast with V-게 만들다 (coercive causative, stronger compulsion) and lexical causatives (more natural for specific verb pairs). 게 하다 is the safe default when unsure which morphological suffix to use.

---

### pattern-149: V-게 만들다
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: V-게 만들다 adds a sense of coercion or unintended outcome compared to V-게 하다. Tone: the caused agent had little choice or the causation is more forceful/emotional.
- **Examples**:
  - 그 소식이 그녀를 울게 만들었어요. (That news forced her to cry / drove her to tears.)
  - 그 책이 저를 생각하게 만들었어요. (That book made me think.)
  - 힘든 상황이 그를 포기하게 만들었어요. (The difficult situation made him give up.)
  - 그의 말이 저를 화나게 만들었어요. (His words made me angry.)
- **Notes**: Use 만들다 when the causing is non-neutral, coercive, or when the outcome was unintended by the causee. Avoid 만들다 in contexts where the causing is cooperative or mutual — use 게 하다 in those cases.

### pattern-150: V-겠어요 / V-겠습니다 (volition)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: 1st-person in-the-moment commitment or service promise. Stronger than -(으)ㄹ 거예요 in formality. Expresses the speaker's firm decision or promise at the moment of speaking. Common in service contexts, formal commitments, job interviews.
- **Examples**:
  - 제가 처리하겠습니다. (I will take care of it. firm promise)
  - 도와드리겠습니다. (I will help you. service promise)
  - 다시는 늦지 않겠습니다. (I will not be late again.)
  - 최선을 다하겠습니다. (I will do my best. formal)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -(으)ㄹ 거예요 (states a future plan) and V-겠네요 (inference from evidence). In service contexts 겠 is pragmatically expected for polite commitment — replacing it with -(으)ㄹ 거예요 sounds informal or abrupt.

### pattern-151: A/V-겠네요 / A/V-겠군요 (inference)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Speaker infers a state from available evidence. Appropriate when inference is confident rather than speculative. With 2nd or 3rd person subject. 겠네요 is slightly warmer/conversational; 겠군요 is more literary/formal.
- **Examples**:
  - 지금쯤 피곤하시겠네요. (You must be tired by now. I can infer.)
  - 거기 많이 춥겠군요. (It must be really cold there.)
  - 아, 그러면 어렵겠네요. (Oh, in that case it must be difficult.)
  - 오래 기다리셨겠군요. (You must have waited a long time.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with -(으)ㄹ 것 같아요 (uncertain guess) — use 겠 when the speaker has solid evidence justifying confident inference. The softened social use of 겠: 죄송하지만 그건 어렵겠습니다 (politely declining, where 겠 makes the refusal less blunt).

### pattern-152: V-았/었으면 좋겠어요 (counterfactual wish)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Past tense marker + -으면. With 좋겠어요: expresses a wish that something were different now (simple counterfactual wish). Despite the past morpheme, refers to present or future unmet conditions. Stronger than present 으면 좋겠어요, which can express open future hopes.
- **Examples**:
  - 좀 더 일찍 알았으면 좋겠어요. (I wish I had known a bit earlier.)
  - 같이 갈 수 있었으면 좋겠어요. (I wish we could go together.)
  - 그때 말했으면 좋겠어요. (I wish I had said it then.)
  - 돈이 더 있었으면 좋겠어요. (I wish I had more money.)
- **Notes**: Contrast with V-았/었더라면 (pattern-153, stronger counterfactual with stated regret and result clause). 았/었으면 좋겠어요 is a complete sentence by itself; 았/었더라면 typically pairs with -았/었을 텐데 or -았/었을 거예요.

### pattern-153: V-았/었더라면 … -았/었을 텐데
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Past marker + -더라면: strongly counterfactual, implies the condition was definitively not met. More formal and literary than -았/었으면. Often paired with -았/었을 텐데 or -았/었을 거예요 in the result clause.
- **Examples**:
  - 그때 포기하지 않았더라면 지금쯤 성공했을 텐데. (If I hadn't given up then, I would probably be successful by now.)
  - 네가 도와주지 않았더라면 실패했을 거야. (If you hadn't helped me, I would have failed.)
  - 일찍 왔더라면 봤을 텐데. (If you had come early, you would have seen it.)
  - 그 말을 했더라면 달라졌을 거예요. (If I had said that, things would have changed.)
- **Notes**: The result clause must also be hypothetical — use -았/었을 텐데 or -았/었을 거예요, not plain -아/어요. Placing 않다 after the conditional suffix is ungrammatical: 하지 않으면더라면 is wrong; correct order is 하지 않았더라면.

### pattern-154: V-기 vs V-는 것 (nominalisation contrast)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Select -기 for abstract or predicate-selected contexts: after 좋아하다, 싫어하다, 바라다, 원하다, 시작하다, 그만두다, 어렵다, 쉽다, and after postpositions 위해, 전에, 때문에. Select -는 것 for specific, observable, or referential propositions: after 보다, 알다, 모르다, 이해하다, 느끼다, and when the noun clause is the topic (은/는).
- **Examples**:
  - 운동하기 어렵지만 계속하고 있어요. (Exercising is difficult, but I keep at it. 기 + 어렵다)
  - 그가 매일 운동하는 것을 알고 있어요. (I know that he exercises every day. 는 것 + 알다)
  - 매일 걷기로 했어요. (I decided to walk every day. 기로 하다 — fixed collocation)
  - 수영하기 좋아해요. (I like swimming. 기 + 좋아하다)
- **Notes**: Certain constructions require -기 regardless of definiteness: 기로 하다 (decide to), 기는 하다 (doing X, but...), 기 때문에 (because of doing). These are fixed collocations — substituting 는 것으로 했어요 is ungrammatical.

### pattern-155: A/V-네요 (fresh realisation, sentence-final)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Attach 네요 directly to the predicate stem as a sentence-final ending. Expresses that the speaker is noticing or realising something for the first time at the moment of speaking. Past form: -았/었네요. Incompatible with previously-known information.
- **Examples**:
  - 한국어가 생각보다 재미있네요. (Korean is more interesting than I thought — I'm just realising this.)
  - 오셨네요. (Oh, you've come! — noticing someone's arrival)
  - 그렇게 됐네요. (So that's how it turned out.)
  - 생각보다 어렵지 않네요. (It's not as hard as I thought.)
- **Notes**: Cross-reference pattern-113 (-네요 as A2 pattern). This B2 entry covers the full pragmatic constraints: -네요 obligatorily signals first-person realisation, not report of fact. Using -네요 for known information or to describe others' experiences is pragmatically inappropriate.

### pattern-156: A/V-군요 / V-는군요 (exclamatory polite)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Stronger surprise or exclamation than 네요; often used when the speaker is more visibly impressed or realising something significant. Formal counterpart of 구나. Adjective/copula: stem+군요. Verb present: 는군요. Past: 았/었군요.
- **Examples**:
  - 벌써 10년이 됐군요! (It's already been 10 years! — surprised exclamation)
  - 한국어를 잘하는군요. (Oh, you speak Korean well!)
  - 많이 바뀌었군요. (Oh, it has changed a lot!)
  - 그렇게 된 거군요. (Oh, so that's how it happened.)
- **Notes**: 군요 is 해요체/합쇼체; 구나 is informal (반말). Cross-reference pattern-114 for the A2 introduction of this pattern.

### pattern-157: V-(으)ㄹ걸요 (regret / hedged assertion)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Dual function: (1) retrospective regret (1st person) — I should have done X; (2) confident hedged assertion — I bet X is the case. Tone determined by evidential context. Cross-reference pattern-115 for the B1 introduction.
- **Examples**:
  - 그때 말할걸요. (I should have said something then. mild regret)
  - 지금쯤 알았을걸요. (They've probably figured it out by now. assertion)
  - 좀 더 열심히 할걸 그랬어요. (I should have worked harder.)
  - 그 사람이 이미 갔을걸요. (I bet that person has already left.)
- **Notes**: The regret reading requires first-person subject in past-implication context; the assertion reading applies to 2nd/3rd person states. -(으)ㄹ걸 (no 요) is the casual form often followed by 그랬어 for emphasis.

### pattern-158: V-았/었을 텐데 (unfulfilled expectation)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Past + -(으)ㄹ 텐데: expresses the speaker's expectation or assumption that a past condition would have held, implying it did not or may not have. Often used as a trailing sentence expressing mild disappointment or gentle regret.
- **Examples**:
  - 일찍 왔으면 봤을 텐데. (If you'd come early, you would have seen it.)
  - 그때 말했으면 달라졌을 텐데. (If you had said it then, things would have been different.)
  - 더 연습했으면 잘 했을 텐데. (If you had practiced more, you would have done well.)
  - 알았으면 도와줬을 텐데. (If I had known, I would have helped.)
- **Notes**: The -았/었 marker is required to anchor the condition in the past — present/future -(으)ㄹ 텐데 does not express unfulfilled past expectation. Often functions as a sentence-final trailing comment without an explicit main clause.

### pattern-159: N이/가 vs N은/는 (discourse contrast)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Use 이/가 when the noun phrase is new to the discourse, answers an implicit 'who/what' question, or exhaustively identifies the subject ('It is specifically N and not others'). Use 은/는 when the noun has been mentioned, is discourse-given, when explicitly contrasting two items, or when stating a general truth about the topic.
- **Examples**:
  - 어제 친구가 전화했어요. (A friend called yesterday. — new info: the call-maker is the friend)
  - 이건 비싸지만 저건 싸요. (This one is expensive, but that one is cheap. — contrast with 은/는)
  - 코끼리는 코가 길어요. (As for elephants, their noses are long. — topic + subject)
  - 누가 했어요? 제가 했어요. (Who did it? I did. — 이/가 for exhaustive identification)
- **Notes**: With predicates 있다/없다/되다/아니다: 이/가 is required regardless of discourse given-ness. Double-marked NPs (N은/는 … 이/가) contain both a topic and a new-information subject: topic sets the frame; 이/가 introduces new information within that frame.

### pattern-160: 따라서 / 그러므로 (formal conclusion connectors)
- **Level**: C1
- **Usage**: Sentence-initial discourse connectors signalling logical conclusion in formal writing. 따라서 ('therefore, accordingly') and 그러므로 ('therefore, for that reason') are interchangeable in most formal contexts; 따라서 is more neutral-formal, 그러므로 slightly more argumentative. Replace spoken 그래서 in academic/journalistic register.
- **Examples**:
  - 실험 결과가 가설을 뒷받침했다. 따라서 이 이론은 유효하다고 볼 수 있다. (The experiment results supported the hypothesis. Therefore, this theory can be considered valid.)
  - 비용이 너무 많다. 그러므로 이 계획은 수정이 필요하다. (The cost is too high. Therefore, this plan needs revision.)
  - 증거가 충분하다. 따라서 결론을 내릴 수 있다. (The evidence is sufficient. Therefore, we can draw a conclusion.)
  - 수요가 줄었다. 그러므로 생산을 줄여야 한다. (Demand has decreased. Therefore, production must be reduced.)
- **Notes**: Do not use spoken 그래서 in formal writing — 그래서 is grammatical but registers as informal. 따라서 and 그러므로 are discourse-level sentence connectors, not conjunctive endings.

### pattern-161: 그러나 / 반면에 / 한편 (formal contrast connectors)
- **Level**: C1
- **Usage**: 그러나 = formal written direct contrast/reversal (formal equivalent of 하지만). 반면에 = explicit two-sided contrast, comparing two entities. 한편 = introduces a parallel or simultaneous development without direct opposition. Do not use spoken 그런데 as a formal contrast connector — 그런데 has a conversational, topic-shifting function.
- **Examples**:
  - 그는 열심히 했다. 그러나 결과가 좋지 않았다. (He worked hard. However, the result was not good.)
  - A국의 경제는 성장했다. 반면에 B국은 침체를 겪었다. (Country A's economy grew. In contrast, Country B experienced stagnation.)
  - 정부는 새 정책을 발표했다. 한편, 시민 단체들은 반발했다. (The government announced a new policy. Meanwhile, civic groups pushed back.)
  - 이 방법은 빠르다. 반면에 저 방법은 정확하다. (This method is fast. On the other hand, that method is accurate.)
- **Notes**: 그러나 = direct reversal; 반면에 = explicit two-sided contrast; 한편 = parallel development without opposition. Confusing 한편 with 반면에 is a common error: 반면에 explicitly opposes; 한편 introduces a parallel or simultaneous development.

### pattern-162: 즉 / 다시 말해서 (written clarification)
- **Level**: C1
- **Usage**: 즉 ('that is, namely') introduces a restatement or specification of the previous clause. 다시 말해서 ('in other words') is slightly more elaborate. Both require that the following clause clarify rather than add new information. Require semantic equivalence or narrowing between clauses.
- **Examples**:
  - 이 계획은 비용이 너무 많이 든다. 즉, 현실적이지 않다는 뜻이다. (This plan costs too much. That is, it is not realistic.)
  - 그는 매일 운동한다. 다시 말해서, 건강 관리를 매우 중시한다. (He exercises every day. In other words, he values health management greatly.)
  - 세 가지 조건이 모두 필요하다. 즉, 어느 하나라도 빠지면 안 된다. (All three conditions are necessary. Namely, none can be missing.)
  - 이것이 핵심이다. 다시 말해서, 가장 중요한 부분이다. (This is the key point. In other words, it is the most important part.)
- **Notes**: Do not use 즉 to introduce new supporting evidence — it requires semantic equivalence or narrowing. Using 즉 where 그리고 (and) or 따라서 (therefore) is needed is a common error.

### pattern-163: 결국 / 궁극적으로 (culmination markers)
- **Level**: C1
- **Usage**: 결국 ('in the end, ultimately') summarises a chain of events or arguments. 궁극적으로 ('ultimately, at the most fundamental level') is used for deeper philosophical or analytical culmination. Both appear at the start of concluding sentences. 결국 can also be a mid-sentence adverb meaning 'in the end'.
- **Examples**:
  - 결국 중요한 것은 노력이다. (In the end, what matters is effort.)
  - 궁극적으로 우리는 같은 목표를 추구한다. (Ultimately, we pursue the same goal.)
  - 여러 시도 끝에 결국 성공했다. (After many attempts, in the end they succeeded.)
  - 궁극적으로 이 문제는 소통의 부재에서 비롯된다. (Ultimately, this problem stems from a lack of communication.)
- **Notes**: Do not confuse 결국 with 드디어 ('finally, after a long wait') — 결국 focuses on logical culmination and often implies inevitability; 드디어 marks temporal completion of an anticipated event.

### pattern-164: ㅋㅋ / ㅠㅠ / ㅇㅇ (consonant emoticons)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Laughter, distress, and affirmation expressed through single consonants or consonant repetition in informal digital communication. ㅋ = slight smile, ㅋㅋ = chuckling, ㅋㅋㅋ = laughing hard. ㅠㅠ / ㅜㅜ = crying/distress/disappointment. ㅇㅇ = informal affirmative (yes/yep/yeah, equivalent to 응/네 in chat). Register-restricted to peer informal communication.
- **Examples**:
  - 그거 진짜요? ㅋㅋㅋ (Is that real? haha / lol)
  - 오늘 시험 망했어 ㅠㅠ (I bombed the exam today T_T)
  - ㅇㅇ 알겠어 ㅋ (Yeah, got it lol)
  - 대박이다 ㅋㅋ (That's insane haha)
- **Notes**: Using 하하 or 히히 in chat contexts reads as stilted or sarcastic. ㅠㅠ belongs strictly in peer informal communication — do not use in email to professor or work communication. ㅇㅇ as affirmative is often followed by more text.

### pattern-165: 완전 / 진짜 / 대박 (colloquial intensifiers)
- **Level**: B2
- **Usage**: Grammaticalised intensifiers and exclamations in informal/digital register. 완전 (originally 'completely') has become a general intensifier: 완전 좋아, 완전 별로야. 진짜 ('really') and 너무 ('too much' → 'really, very' in informal speech) function similarly. 대박 (literally 'jackpot'): positive wow/amazing, also ironically for bad news. 헐: shocked or deadpan disbelief. All three are register-restricted to peer speech and digital contexts.
- **Examples**:
  - 그 드라마 완전 재미있어. 진짜 너무 좋아. (That drama is totally fun. I really love it so much.)
  - 헐 걔가 그랬어? 대박이다. (Oh wow, they did that? That's insane.)
  - 완전 힘들었어. (I was totally exhausted.)
  - 진짜요? 대박! (Really? Wow!)
- **Notes**: 너무 has two registers: standard 'excessively' (너무 비싸요 = too expensive) and informal 'really/very' (너무 좋아 = I really like it). Do not use these intensifiers in formal writing where 매우/상당히 are required. 대박 in modern usage is a general exclamation, not a gambling term.
